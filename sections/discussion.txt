Threats to validity. One game, one budget, one population size. We 
tuned a simplest slot game there is against three stats, more complex game 
such as cascade or cluster games , with more bonus other than free game 
such as hold and spin may favour other methods. So this game stats could be 
calculated exactly with analytical analysis with out the need of simulation, which 
was not to keep the approach general. Every
run had 1000 evaluations and μ = 10. A larger budget might let crossover pay
off, and a larger population gives more diversity but fewer generations; we tested
neither. 

Untuned methods. The GA used textbook defaults, and the annealing
temperatures (0.14 to 0.002) were set once and never changed. Tuning could
change the order among the tied methods, so our claim is about sensible defaults.

Best m chosen on the same seeds. When each method is compared at its own
best m, the same 30 seeds choose m and measure the result. This favours the GA,
whose best value is the lowest of 36 configurations (9 × 4 values of m) against 4
for simulated annealing. The base-stage draw holds despite this, and at a fixed
m = 5 it is also a draw.

Weighted-sum error and conflicting free targets. he

less, so the free stage compares how methods trade one target against another.
We did not prove that the free targets cannot all be met; the exact calculation
could settle this.

Noise floor. Its standard deviations (0.034 and 0.10) come from
a measurement made before the main experiment, and are above every value in
the final noise study. So the floor can turn a small real difference into a tie, but
it does not add findings. The noise study measured only the best reel set of each
method and m (96 reel sets), not every run.
