A video slot's main core structure, its reel strips, controls how much a game pays and how the game feels. Slot mathematicians tune these reel strips by hand and run millions of simulated spins after each change, which is time taking and tedidious process . So we wanted to test whether a GA algorithm compared against local search can auto tune the reel towards desired result. We ask whether a genetic algorithm (GA) can outperform local search. For this, on one slot game, we have compared 9 configurations of GA with hill climbing, simulated annealing and random search, all of them with an equal number of evaluations and 4 mutation strengths, run over 30 paired seeds. In the base stage, no GA configuration could significantly beat local search, with the best GA having a median error of 0.076 against simulated annealing's 0.075. Generational replacement is significantly worse, losing to steady-state replacement on 30 of 30 seeds at the highest mutation strength ($A_{12} = 1.00$). In the free stage, no GA beats simulated annealing, and the targets appear to conflict.
